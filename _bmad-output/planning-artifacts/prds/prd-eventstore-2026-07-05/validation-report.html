Poor

This is a technically substantive and unusually candid requirements catalogue that supports one immediate decision well: keep implementation readiness blocked. It is not safe as the authoritative chain-top baseline it claims to be because downstream usability is broken by an unreconciled artifact set and unavailable normative OQ8 source, while acceptance clarity and shape remain thin. The explicit Reject posture must stay in force until those gaps close.

The platform-contract review materially strengthens that conclusion with current repository evidence: generated REST tenant handling and command-status Location behavior contradict adopted public-boundary rules, silent append loss remains unguarded, and compatibility, authentication-conformance, and consumer-removal contracts are incomplete. The previous reject remains directionally correct, but its baseline and reasons do not describe one coherent current repository snapshot.

Decision-readiness

adequate

The PRD makes the current stop decision unmistakable: document finality is separate from readiness, the bound result is Reject, and §0 forbids release, deployment, migration, and downstream handoff. The blocker register is owned and trigger-bound, but the eventual go decision lacks a single executable exit contract.

The future READY decision has no single executable exit contract

§12 OR17
A reviewer must reconstruct mandatory gates, evidence, evaluators, waiver rules, and current results across several sections.
Fix: Add one Phase 4 exit table with a pass/fail row for each gate and durable approval evidence.

Substance over theater

strong

The content is earned. The vision is specific to a DAPR-native event-sourcing platform, roles directly drive SDK, API, UI, release, operator, and evidence requirements, and NFRs carry product-specific limits. Known failures and negative evidence are retained instead of being presented as success.

Strategic coherence

adequate

The PRD has a clear thesis: platform reuse and operational hardening must ship together. Metrics test both halves and counter-metrics prevent shortcuts. The minimum slice, however, is still described mostly as the inherited Epic 1–7 inventory rather than as a thesis-derived priority order.

medium

MVP priority follows the epic inventory more than the product thesis

§9.1
The scope does not identify which outcomes are indispensable to validating the reuse-and-hardening bet versus supporting readiness work.
Fix: Add a short scope-logic paragraph grouping mandatory MVP outcomes by the thesis they validate.

Scope honesty

strong

Scope is unusually honest. MVP, exclusions, and committed post-MVP work are separated; append fencing remains a blocker rather than a waiver; planning/evidence outcomes are not misrepresented as implementation; and open refinements have owners and triggers.

Downstream usability

broken

The glossary, requirement IDs, feature grouping, and traceability aids are locally strong. Yet the PRD itself records that PRD, architecture, UX, epics, and lifecycle evidence do not form one approved baseline. Safety-sensitive OQ8 rules are also governed by external bytes unavailable inside EventStore.

critical

Planning artifacts are not one approved baseline

§11.3; OR14, OR15
Matching PRD/architecture hashes in epics were refreshed without the required approval sequence; architecture remains draft, lifecycle sources conflict, and UX hashes are stale.
Fix: Reconcile and approve the planning set atomically, then run guarded drift and lifecycle validations before handoff.
critical

Normative OQ8 requirements are unavailable inside the evidence boundary

§1.1; OR11
A digest without accessible governed bytes cannot let downstream workflows extract or validate the overriding state machine, timers, tombstones, public errors, and evidence denominators.
Fix: Retain accessible immutable normative content or a verifiable attestation and make absence or mismatch fail closed.
