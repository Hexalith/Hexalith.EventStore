## Measured mechanism and candidates

The [first evaluation packet](runs-2026-10-21-concurrency-safety/) preserves the unchanged retained candidate's [controlled failure](runs-2026-10-21-concurrency-safety/retained-candidate-concurrency-red.log). Each test establishes a real held actor invocation, reminder or two-message stream on a Ready pooled channel. Another request receives `Unavailable` deliberately returned by the healthy server. Eviction closes the shared channel: all three held RPC attempts end with `Canceled: grpc: the client connection is closing`; the stream stops after one message. The pristine upstream-router overlay passes the same held-user controls. These are remote-attempt observations, not proof of every outer resiliency or scheduler outcome.

Pinned Dapr `b32a815fa2260983e181b95272ea555fe17d3895` shares the address pool across actor calls, reminders and streams. Its pool `Destroy` closes regardless of reference count, and pinned grpc-go `1.83.2` `ClientConn.Close` tears down every underlying transport. A server-returned status on a Ready channel is distinct from a channel in TransientFailure after a real failed connection; both paths have separate controls.

An initial state-gated `ResetConnectBackoff` alternative preserved active RPCs and passed fixed-endpoint integration (focused 1/1, complete class 6/6). However, the [manual-resolver probe](runs-2026-10-21-concurrency-safety/pinned-reset-resolver-churn.log) measured a data race and fatal concurrent map iteration/write: grpc-go `clientconn.go:1183` ranges `cc.conns` after unlocking, while `newAddrConnLocked:931` and `removeAddrConn:943` mutate it. This was a controlled dependency-API probe using the same state gate, not an observed integrated-runtime crash. The reset source/executable and all its passes and failures remain preserved; it is rejected for broader safety. No dependency was patched or updated.

The [second packet](runs-2026-10-21-concurrency-retirement/) evaluates a runtime-only [retirement correction](runs-2026-10-21-concurrency-retirement/correction-from-retained.patch). After `Unavailable`, only an active caller whose channel is observed in TransientFailure marks that channel retired. New leases exclude it; existing leases retain it until their final release. Marking retirement does not decrement the retiring caller's reference. Its existing deferred release runs once, and final closure happens outside the pool lock. Healthy-server `Unavailable` on Ready does not retire the channel. No `ResetConnectBackoff` call remains.

The live drain tests invoke manager retirement directly while actor/reminder/stream calls are held. Router status/state/context classification is tested separately. The pool tests cover idempotent retirement, idle closure, exact last-release closure, and 50 iterations of 32 concurrent leases with concurrent acquisition, retirement and release. Post-class race checks also retire 25 channels beside 25,000 resolver updates. They qualify these controlled interleavings, not every runtime profile or long-lived stream workload. Long-lived users keep their retired channel until they finish; no new forced timeout is introduced.

Both original cancellation/deadline controls and the retained review's cancelled-Unavailable control pass. The new TransientFailure cancellation control is discriminating: removing only `ctx.Err() == nil` fails channel retention, while the old same-channel review control still passes that mutant. [Mutation evidence](runs-2026-10-21-concurrency-retirement/retirement-cancel-guard-mutation-red.log) remains separate. Context checks are not claimed to be atomic with cancellation occurring afterward.
